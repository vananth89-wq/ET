Chapter 1

The month is the unit

Everything starts on the calendar. Before you record anything, it pays to know what the grid is already telling you.

Time Sheet for September 2026 · 26 Aug – 25 Sep
Why it starts on the 26th

A month here runs 26th to 25th

September 2026 means 26 August through 25 September. The first row of the calendar belongs to the previous calendar month, which surprises people once and never again. The four figures under the title — employee, work schedule, planned, recorded — are the whole month in one line.

Greyed columns are your non-working days. On the General schedule that's Friday and Saturday, which means Sunday is an ordinary working day.

Reading a day

The bar under each day is the story

Each cell shows what you logged against what you were scheduled — 8/8h, 0/8h, 10/8h — and the strip along the bottom says the same in colour: pale for partial, green for complete, a red tail for anything past your planned hours, blue for leave.

Days you didn't work

Leave and holidays look different on purpose

An absence fills the cell with a coloured card naming the leave type. A public holiday does the same in purple, and it arrives from the holiday calendar — you don't record it and you can't remove it.

Neither one is something you have to action. They are there so the missing hours make sense to whoever reads the month after you.

Chapter 2

Recording a day

Click any day and a panel opens on the right. Everything you do to that day happens in this panel.

Day panel · Add Entry
Type first, then project

A project entry is made of activities

The Attendance Type decides the shape of the form. Types that need a project — Work, On-Site Visit, Cross-project Support — ask for a project and an Activities & hours list. Types that don't, like Training, just ask for hours.

That difference matters later. An entry's hours are the sum of its activities, not a number you type separately — so when a total looks wrong, it is an activity that is wrong.

Billable or not

The one choice with consequences downstream

On projects that track it, each activity is marked Billable or Not billable. That single flag drives the billable split on your summary, on the project reports, and on the version your project manager sees. Worth a moment's thought rather than a reflex.

Not every project asks. The question is a property of the project, not of the form — internal projects never bill a client, so it simply doesn't appear and those hours land under Internal on your summary. If you expected the choice and don't see it, the project is the reason.

Everything else is reversible. The pencil edits and the bin deletes right up until the month is submitted — nothing you record is final before that.

Chapter 3

More than one entry a day

Real days are rarely one thing. The timesheet expects that — with one rule about what counts as the same thing twice.

Wednesday 16 September · two entries
Adding a second entry

You get told, not stopped

A day that already has something on it shows an amber note above the form. It is information, not a barrier — saving works normally.

On screen "This day already has 1 entry. Your new entry will be added alongside it."
The one real limit

Same type, same project, same day — once

You can put four different things on one day. What you can't do is record the same attendance type against the same project twice on the same date. If you try, the save is refused and you're pointed at the entry that already exists.

The rule holds for types with no project too: two separate Training entries on one day collapse into the same conflict. To add hours to something already recorded, edit it — or let Copy Day merge them for you, which is exactly what the next chapter is about.

Over your plan

Going past 8 hours is allowed and visible

Two entries totalling ten hours against an eight-hour plan give you 10 / 8 hr and a plain statement: +2 hr over plan. Nothing is blocked. The day's bar grows a red tail so the extra reads at a glance from the calendar.

There is a hard ceiling, though — 16 hours in one day. That one is refused outright, and the message names both totals so you can see what tipped it.

Chapter 4

Leave and part-days

Absence sits on the same day panel, behind its own button — and it changes what else the day will accept.

Monday 21 September · Annual Leave
A full day off

Two sentences are the whole rule

Once a full-day absence is on a day, the attendance buttons go and the panel explains itself: "Annual Leave covers the full day — attendance cannot be added." and "Only one absence is allowed per day."

The buttons disappear rather than letting you fill in a form that was always going to be refused. A full-day type also fills in your planned hours and locks them — part days are switched on per type by an administrator, not by you.

Half days

A part-day absence still leaves room to work

Record four hours of a half-day-capable leave type against an eight-hour plan and Add Attendance stays available for the rest. That's the case the design exists for — a morning at the clinic and an afternoon at your desk is one ordinary day, recorded as two things.

It works the other way too. If attendance is already on the day, only a part-day absence can join it, and the panel says so before you open anything.

Chapter 5

Filling a week in one go

Nobody wants to record the same eight hours nine times. Two tools exist for this, and they solve different problems.

Create · Copy Day
Create · shortcut C

Which tool for which job

Create is for one entry repeated across many dates — the same eight hours on nine days. Copy Day is for carrying a day's whole shape somewhere else, entries and activities intact. If the days differ, neither helps; record them one at a time.

All open days to date selects everything still empty up to today. It is usually the fastest way back from a week away.

The greyed-out dates

Future days depend on what you picked

That isn't a bug — it's the advance-dating rule drawn for you. Project work can't be recorded before it happens, so future days switch off the moment you choose Work. Choose Training and they light up again, because a booked course next Tuesday is a fact worth recording now.

Weekends and holidays stay selectable throughout; working on one is unusual, not impossible.

The one to watch

Matching entries merge silently

Paste or create onto a day that already holds a match — same type, same project, same activity — and the two become one. A copied 8h Training landing on an existing 2h Training leaves a single entry reading 10h. A project entry brings its activities in alongside the ones already listed.

Worth a second look Nothing on screen says two entries became one — just a larger number. If you paste onto the wrong day, check the total before moving on. Every paste carries an Undo; use it.
When it refuses

A refused paste changes nothing

If any part of a paste can't be applied — a genuine clash, a future date, the 16-hour ceiling — the whole thing is rejected and the target day is left exactly as it was. You will never find half a paste sitting on a day.

Leave and public holidays never travel. If the source day had leave on it, the toast tells you it was left behind.

Chapter 6

Reading your month back

The Summary tab is the one screen worth opening before you submit. It answers "am I done?" better than counting green bars.

Monthly Summary · September 2026
The top row

Nine numbers, and one that matters most

Recorded against planned, what's remaining, your average on the days you were present — then days logged, missing, and leave. Missing is the one to watch: it counts only past working days with nothing on them, so it's a real to-do list rather than a reminder that the month isn't over.

On the right, the productivity split — billable, not billable, internal, support given — computed from the billable flag you set on each activity.

Week by week

Where the gaps actually are

Each week carries its own badge: Partial, Over by 2h, In progress. Below them, Days needing time lists the specific past days you still owe, as clickable chips — and a line telling you whether today has anything on it yet.

That list is the fastest route to a finished month. Work the chips until they're gone.

Where the time went

By project and type

One ring for the whole month, with leave and non-project types shown alongside the projects. Underneath, Attainment against your planned hours, and a plain-language line telling you the daily average needed to finish the month on target.

The detail

Down to the activity

Every project broken into its activities, ranked, with the billable split called out per project. This is the view your project manager sees a version of — if an activity name here looks wrong or vague, it's worth fixing before you submit.

Chapter 7

Handing it in

One button ends the month. It is reversible, and it's worth knowing exactly what changes.

Submit for Approval
The confirmation

It shows you the total first

The dialog states what you are about to send — 124 hr across 17 entries — and what happens next. If that total isn't what you expected, this is the moment to cancel and go back to the Summary tab.

After submitting

The toolbar changes, and that's the signal

The status chip turns to Pending Approval, a Submitted date appears beside your hours, and Create, Copy Day and Submit are replaced by a single Withdraw. Export PDF stays.

While it's out

Read-only, not frozen

Open any day and the entries are still there to read, with the edit and delete controls gone and one line explaining why.

On screen "Waiting for approval — withdraw it to make changes."

Withdraw, fix, submit again. If your approver returns the month instead, it unlocks the same way and their reason comes with it.

Chapter 8

"Why did it say that?"

The messages that stop you are short, and each one has exactly one cause. Find the sentence you saw.

"<Project> is already recorded on this day. Edit that entry instead."

Same type, same project, twice

One entry per day per attendance type per project. A second one has nowhere to go.

Do this: open the entry already on the day and change its hours, or add the time under a different type.

"Attendance cannot be recorded in advance."

The date is in the future

Most types can only record work that has happened. Types that are genuinely scheduled ahead — Training, for instance — are allowed, and your administrator decides which.

Do this: wait for the day, or check whether the right type for this is one that permits advance dating.

"<Leave name> covers the full day — attendance cannot be added."

A whole-day absence is on the date

There are no hours left to record against. The Add Attendance button is removed rather than allowed to fail.

Do this: if you did work, the absence is the thing that's wrong — reduce it to a part day, then record the hours.

"Only one absence is allowed per day."

The day already carries an absence

Two absence types on one date can't be reconciled, so the second is refused.

Do this: edit the existing absence, or delete it and record the one you actually meant.

"One or more of the selected entries cannot be recorded in advance."

A paste or bulk create included a future date

The check runs across everything you selected, not the day as a whole — so one project entry in the selection is enough to stop it.

Do this: untick the entry that can't be advance-dated, or choose an earlier target day.

"Attendance is already recorded on this day, so only a part-day absence can be added."

You're adding leave to a day you worked

Not an error — a narrowing. The absence type picker will only offer types that can be taken as part of a day.

Do this: nothing, if a half day is what you meant. If the whole day was leave, remove the attendance first.
